# Story draft — Last Known

Status: proposed narrative, revised for decades of abandonment and the epidemic setting. The sibling relationship and ending remain proposals. Character names are deliberately unset. Contains the full mystery and ending.

## Premise

A former communications technician returns to his abandoned home city after discovering evidence that his younger sister survived the evacuation in which he believed she died. He reconstructs her movements by recovering physical and digital records.

He returns to learn whether she waited for him. He discovers what she chose to do after she stopped waiting.

## Before the collapse

The siblings grew up above their father's electronics repair shop. After his death, the sister kept the shop running. Her brother maintained communications infrastructure and helped support her financially, but was rarely present.

She wanted to study elsewhere. He kept postponing a conversation about selling the shop, treating it as something that held their family together. She experienced it as an obligation she had inherited.

One of their last ordinary disagreements concerned her acceptance letter. He repaired her laptop but failed to ask about the future she was planning on it. Their estrangement grows from repeated small absences, not one conveniently cruel sentence.

## The evacuation

As the epidemic spreads, transport becomes intermittent and emergency services consolidate. The siblings agree to meet at the city's northern railway station if they lose contact.

The protagonist stays at an exchange to restore a failed emergency communications link. He sends a message: “Stay there. I'm coming.” It never reaches her.

He reaches the station after its final evacuation movement. Her canvas bag remains in the waiting room. Conflicting closure notices and blocked access prevent him from tracing her onward journey. He eventually leaves with another evacuation group.

He believes she died during the evacuation, but has no confirmation. The detailed chronology and his attempts to search must be developed so his departure feels credible.

## The intervening decades

The city is abandoned for approximately 25–30 years. The protagonist, a young adult during the collapse, is now roughly in his late forties or fifties. Exact ages depend on the final timeline.

He repairs equipment in a surviving community outside the city. A lack of reliable routes and records prevented a successful search. We still need a specific account of previous attempts and why access is possible now; elapsed time alone does not explain his absence.

## Inciting evidence

A recently recovered municipal storage unit reaches his workshop. Its archive includes a file transferred from his sister's shop after the station closed.

The file is a photograph showing her in a reflection inside the shop, with temporary evacuation markings that corroborate its place in the chronology. The transfer timestamp provides a lead; the image supplies independent evidence that she left the station alive.

He returns carrying the recovered data, old service keys, and her canvas bag. The playable story begins on the approach to the city, with no inhabited settlement scene.

## Act I — The shop

The player reaches the family shop and restores a selected computer using salvaged parts and a portable power source. Saved files and repair records show that the sister returned after the station evacuation and helped charge batteries and repair radios.

Upstairs, her belongings show that she had already been preparing to leave before the crisis. The room establishes a life and ambitions beyond the protagonist.

A list of shelter names and revised addresses points toward the station's overflow evacuation arrangements.

Question answered: she survived the station closure.

New question: why did she return to the city instead of leaving?

## Act II — The station

A recoverable fragment of silent footage shows her waiting, then helping another evacuee toward an onward collection point. She leaves her bag to free her hands. She looks back at the entrance before leaving.

Her abandoned belongings were never proof of her death. The player corroborates the destination through a closure notice and transport records.

In a first-person scene, the protagonist opens the bag. It contains clothes, a portable music player, and her acceptance letter. We must establish why he has preserved rather than thoroughly examined it previously, or move this discovery to her room during script refinement.

The next lead is a school converted into a temporary shelter.

## Act III — The registry

At the school, the player discovers a makeshift registry tracking separated evacuees: who was present, where they went, and who remained missing.

The sister helped maintain it using donated and repaired equipment. Fragments lead through a clinic and a telephone exchange. Each location adds evidence of her choices and provides corroboration for the next destination.

Her account appears active at incompatible locations. Comparing local records and handover notes establishes that others used her equipment and that some transfers were queued. Account activity cannot safely be treated as her personal movements.

The player learns her habits, humour, frustration, and aspirations through small details, not only through evidence of sacrifice.

Question answered: she returned to help people whose onward destinations had been lost.

New question: which records actually establish her final movements?

## Act IV — The undelivered message

At the exchange, the player recovers the failed message queue. “Stay there. I'm coming” was never delivered.

The protagonist's belief that she died obeying him was mistaken. That does not erase the earlier distance between them.

Separate records show that his repair briefly restored the school's connection. The siblings helped the same evacuation effort without knowing it.

The registry's final handover points to an elevated municipal radio relay. Its exact form follows the chosen city's infrastructure; an observatory is only one possible architectural treatment.

## Act V — The last relay

The sister reached the relay with the remaining evacuation group. She and another volunteer stayed to complete a registry transmission after transport departed, expecting to leave along a maintenance route.

Proposed outcome: both later died before reaching safety. The cause must follow the revised setting and chosen terrain. The previous storm-and-landslide ending is removed; an exact replacement is not yet established.

Three independent records resolve the investigation: a local transmission record, an acknowledgement confirming receipt and use of the registry, and a later search or burial record establishing her fate and grave location. Each needs a credible survival and discovery path.

The player learns her fate before reaching the grave, allowing the final walk to carry the emotional weight.

## Proposed ending

The protagonist finds her marker and clears the vegetation from her name. He sits beside it and reads the acceptance letter in full.

An optional repaired music player can provide a quiet instrumental passage. The scene must work without it. No apparition, spoken forgiveness, or last-minute reversal appears.

He copies the surviving registry and updates her entry with the confirmed information. Other entries remain unresolved. He leaves carrying information that may answer questions for other surviving families.

The emotional resolution is recognition: he finally understands a person whose life he had reduced to the moment he lost her.

## Narrative constraints and next decisions

All playable scenes and cinematics preserve first-person anonymity. Text is allowed; spoken dialogue is absent. Historical footage can show people without adding present-day NPC encounters.

Before treating this story as final, decide:

1. Whether the sister relationship and proposed death ending are right.
2. The city, names, ages, and exact elapsed time.
3. What prevented an earlier return and what changes now.
4. The final relay's role, the sister's fate, and the corroborating evidence.
5. A dated evacuation timeline that makes every clue and journey consistent.

Do not use the rejected character names, the fifteen-year interval, crop failures, or a single storm as established story facts.
