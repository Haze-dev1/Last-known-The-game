# Realistic city map brief

The map should feel like a functioning city that was abandoned and changed over decades.

**Decision (4 October 2026): the game uses one shared, geographically grounded Kowloon world.** It covers the five Kowloon districts (Kowloon plus New Kowloon to the Kowloon ridge) and is built from LandsD terrain and building surveys and OpenStreetMap. Chapters are narrative progression inside this world, not geographic limits. The pipeline, sources, coverage polygon and limitations are in [the world map pipeline](../world-map-pipeline.md). The guidance below still applies to how places are adapted for play.

## Direction

Use a real city or a coherent portion of one as the geographic reference. Preserve the relationships that make it believable: terrain, water, road hierarchy, rail corridors, neighbourhood patterns, building access, and infrastructure.

Kowloon is the selected geographic reference. The user prefers a believable adaptation rather than complete fidelity. Preserve plausible connections and travel distances while adapting selected streets and interiors for gameplay. Sham Shui Po is the proposed starting neighbourhood inside the whole-Kowloon world.

## Select geography before landmarks

The reference city should support a dense urban core, residential areas, transport infrastructure, and a climate compatible with the intended vegetation. Elevation or distinct landmarks should help players orient themselves without permanent waypoints.

Do not select a city only because it has a recognizable skyline. Check its street-level character, ordinary buildings, service access, drainage, and neighbourhood transitions. Cultural identity then informs names, signage, interiors, and the siblings' lives.

Scale is real metric scale. The playable exterior boundary is the documented Kowloon coverage polygon; interiors and access changes remain per-location design work.

## Preserve urban logic

| Layer | Design requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Terrain and water | Establish elevations, slopes, waterways, and low points before assigning floods or routes. |
| Streets | Retain a readable hierarchy of main roads, local streets, alleys, and service access. |
| Rail and transit | Give stations credible tracks, approaches, service areas, and pedestrian connections. |
| Buildings | Respect entrances, stairs, floor heights, party walls, loading access, and courtyards. |
| Land use | Connect housing to schools, shops, clinics, and transit; give industry appropriate access. |
| Utilities | Place cabinets, substations, exchanges, and relay sites for credible service needs. |
| Growth | Tie vegetation to local species, exposure, water, soil, and nearby sources. |
| Damage | Record a cause for important collapses, obstructions, or inaccessible areas. |

Real map data can establish geography, but it does not automatically provide reliable interiors, utility routes, or current building heights. Those layers require additional references or explicitly fictional design.

## Two versions of the same place

For each playable block, describe how it worked before abandonment and how it works now. For example, a service alley once served shops; after decades, a fallen tree blocks one end, a courtyard provides a detour, and an intact stairwell leads to an overlooking roof.

Avoid placing a rooftop bridge wherever progression needs one. Vertical routes must account for gaps, height differences, access, and the protagonist's age and physical capabilities. His movement style is not yet finalized.

## Abandonment treatment

Use varied conditions rather than a uniform green overlay. Protected interiors, sunlit rooms, parks, drainage channels, elevated roads, and underground spaces should look different.

Keep large portions of the original city legible. Decades of abandonment do not automatically justify a jungle inside every room or rubble beneath every tower. Specific ecological and structural outcomes need reference research for the selected location.

The timing of evacuation also varies: an ordinary apartment, a late shelter, and a final relay should preserve different phases of the collapse.

## Provisional story locations

These are narrative functions, not fixed coordinates or approved final POIs.

| Location | Earlier urban function | Investigation role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Family repair shop and home | Local commercial frontage with dwelling above | Establish the sister's life and prove she returned. |
| Northern station | Passenger rail and evacuation interchange | Reconstruct her departure and revise the meaning of the abandoned bag. |
| School shelter | Neighbourhood school temporarily repurposed | Introduce the missing-person registry. |
| Clinic | Local healthcare and evacuation support | Corroborate movements and registry handovers. |
| Telephone exchange | Communications service for connected neighbourhoods | Recover the failed message and trace the final transfer. |
| Elevated relay | Municipal communications site | Resolve the transmission, final journey, and proposed ending. |

Arrange these according to the city's geography and evacuation chronology. Optional locations can supply supplies, context, or corroboration without making every building part of the same mystery.

## Navigation and exploration

Support navigation through street names, landmarks, addresses, paper maps, and recovered plans. Give alternate physical routes where geography supports them. A clue should explain why a place matters before the player commits to a difficult journey.

Define distances only after testing traversal time. A physically large map is not required for a believable city. Background neighbourhoods can imply the wider metropolis, but playable boundaries need understandable geography or access conditions.

## Research and next deliverable

After choosing a candidate city, inspect authoritative maps, terrain and flood information, urban plans, architectural references, and local vegetation evidence. Potential base-map sources include OpenStreetMap and municipal open-data portals; availability, accuracy, licensing, and attribution must be checked before use.

The streamed world now provides terrain and water, roads, rail, district boundaries, building masses and an overview map. The next map step is placing the provisional POIs at real candidate sites and measuring walking routes between them. Fictional changes (blocked streets, access, damage) must be recorded separately from source geography.
