# World and creative direction

This document records the setting for a solitary survival investigation game. Updated 3 October 2026.

## Agreed foundations

- Post-apocalyptic urban setting with a realistic city structure.
- Ordinary vegetation reclaims the city over decades. There is no accelerated biological growth.
- First-person perspective throughout; the protagonist's face remains unseen.
- No present-day human NPCs or spoken dialogue. Historical people can appear in photographs and silent footage.
- Eerie solitude, melancholy, and curiosity; horror is not the intended tone.
- Survival, exploration, and deduction support the same journey.
- Computers, storage devices, and networking infrastructure provide evidence and access.
- Environmental storytelling and first-person cinematic sequences carry the story, with sparse music and detailed ambient sound.

## Working setting

A global epidemic causes widespread deaths and evacuation. The city empties unevenly as services lose staff and supplies. Some buildings are abandoned early; temporary shelters and evacuation infrastructure remain active longer.

The immediate epidemic is long past when the game begins. Surviving communities have consolidated elsewhere. This city has not been resettled at scale. The specific reasons it remains inaccessible or unattractive to settlers need to be developed once its geography is chosen.

The working interval is 25–30 years since abandonment, in a warm, humid region. These are design assumptions, not a verified ecological forecast. The exact interval, climate, vegetation, and condition of structures must be checked against the eventual reference city.

The epidemic explains the human absence. Time, climate, water, and lack of maintenance explain the overgrowth. Crop failure and a single catastrophic storm are no longer the story's foundation.

## Visual logic

Vegetation varies by water, sunlight, soil, exposure, and nearby seed sources. Parks, waterways, gardens, and rail corridors guide the visual design of its spread. Sheltered rooms can remain comparatively bare while broken, damp rooms support growth.

Use recognizable urban fabric beneath the vegetation: road hierarchies, pavement, drainage, utility access, building entrances, service yards, and neighbourhood boundaries. Avoid covering every facade in the same vines or assuming every tower has collapsed.

Flooded areas require a local explanation such as low elevation and failed drainage. Structural damage requires a credible cause and will need reference work. These are art-direction requirements rather than claims about a specific city's future.

## Technology after decades

Most equipment is unusable on discovery. The player repairs selected devices, supplies power, and recovers fragments from protected storage. Surviving information is uneven and sometimes incomplete.

No ordinary battery has continuously powered a computer for decades. Any surviving autonomous installation needs an explicit energy and maintenance history. A recovered desktop represents saved files or recoverable state, not a computer mysteriously retaining an unsaved session through decades without power.

Progression focuses on isolated devices and small, useful network segments. Restoring an entire municipal grid is not an established capability. Long-term storage survival and repair details remain research tasks.

## Narrative principles

Give the player an understandable immediate objective while gradually revealing its personal significance. Avoid artificial amnesia or hiding evidence the character is visibly reading.

Evidence should support deductions. Account activity alone does not establish who operated a device, and timestamps may need corroboration. The central mystery must have a discoverable answer.

Survival creates expedition choices without constantly interrupting investigation. Exact resource systems, threats, death consequences, and difficulty remain undecided.
